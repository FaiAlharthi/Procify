# RFP Writing Guide - The Five Criteria

A practical guide for writing each of the five standard RFP sections, with guidance on what good content looks like and common mistakes to avoid.

## 1. Objective

**Purpose:** Tell the reader, in a few sentences, what is being procured and why.

**Good objective includes:**
- The subject of procurement (system, equipment, service)
- The scale (quantity, number of users, duration)
- The business reason driving the need
- Any critical ongoing obligation (e.g., support, maintenance)

**Common mistakes:**
- Too vague ("We need a better system") - doesn't specify scope or scale
- Too long - objective should be a short paragraph, not a full requirements list
- Missing the "why" - readers should understand the business driver, not just the ask

## 2. Scope of Work

**Purpose:** Define exactly what the vendor must deliver, in enough detail that any qualified vendor can determine if they can fulfill it.

**Good scope of work includes:**
- Clear subcategories (e.g., by functional area)
- Specific, measurable requirements per subcategory
- Technical specifications where relevant (hardware, software standards, formats)
- A mix of mandatory and optional/nice-to-have items, clearly distinguished

**Common mistakes:**
- Listing requirements as one long undifferentiated paragraph
- Using subjective language ("user-friendly", "modern") without defining what that means functionally
- Omitting integration or interoperability needs

## 3. Technical Criteria

**Purpose:** Define the non-functional requirements - security, performance, compliance - that the solution must meet.

**Good technical criteria includes:**
- Security requirements (encryption, access control, authentication)
- Compliance/regulatory requirements relevant to the jurisdiction or industry
- Data handling requirements (residency, retention, post-contract handling)
- Scalability and deployment flexibility requirements
- Vendor security posture expectations (certifications, incident history disclosure)

## 4. Commercial Template

**Purpose:** Set the rules for how vendors must structure their commercial/business response.

**Good commercial template includes:**
- Required pricing structure (e.g., itemized, per-user, flat-rate)
- Required supporting plans (security plan, incident response, BCP/DR)
- Legal/IP/privacy compliance requirements
- Expected SLA commitments

## 5. Project Delivery & Deadlines

**Purpose:** Set expectations for timeline, delivery milestones, and ongoing support.

**Good project delivery section includes:**
- Overall delivery timeline within the contract period
- Support model expectations (dedicated contact, response times)
- Ongoing maintenance/update commitments
- Total contract term/duration

## General Tips
1. Be specific, not generic. Every requirement should be testable.
2. Use consistent formatting. Headings, bullet points, and consistent terminology make the RFP easier to evaluate.
3. Keep vendor neutrality. Never reference specific vendors or products by name within the RFP itself.
4. Match detail to risk. High-risk or high-cost items deserve more detailed requirements.
5. Review for completeness using the five-section checklist before finalizing.
