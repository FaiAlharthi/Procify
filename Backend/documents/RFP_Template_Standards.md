# RFP Template & Structure Standards

This document defines the standard structure and category breakdown for writing a Request for Proposal (RFP), based on best-practice technology procurement formats. It covers what each of the five core sections should contain and the level of detail expected in each.

## The Five Core Sections of an RFP

### 1. Objective
States the overall purpose of the procurement in 2-4 sentences. Should answer:
- What is being procured (system, service, product)?
- Why is it needed (business driver, scaling need, replacement, compliance)?
- What is the high-level outcome expected from the selected vendor?

### 2. Scope of Work
Describes in detail what the vendor is expected to deliver. Common subcategories include:
- User Management & Administration - roles, permissions, hierarchies, dashboards
- User Interface & Experience - branding, mobile compatibility, accessibility standards (e.g., WCAG)
- Content/Service Management - core functional capabilities, versioning, workflows
- Reporting, Dashboards & Analytics - real-time/scheduled reports, export formats, custom fields
- Integration & Interoperability - third-party systems, APIs, data sync requirements
- AI/Automation capabilities (if applicable) - personalization, automated processes

### 3. Technical Criteria
Defines the non-functional and technical compliance requirements. Organized by theme:
- Security & Privacy - encryption (at rest/in transit), data residency, compliance frameworks (e.g., ISO 27001, SOC2)
- Authentication - SSO, MFA requirements
- Vendor Compliance - industry certifications, cybersecurity best practices
- Incident & Continuity Planning - incident response plan, business continuity/disaster recovery (BCP/DR)
- Data Governance - retention policy, post-contract data handling, storage location options
- Scalability & Deployment - elastic scaling, load balancing, deployment flexibility (cloud/on-prem/hybrid)
- Vulnerability Management - pen testing cadence, patch management, disclosure programs
- Third-Party Disclosure - subcontractor involvement, prior security incidents

### 4. Commercial Template
Covers the business and legal terms expected from the vendor's proposal:
- Pricing/cost structure expectations (transparent, itemized)
- Required compliance plans (security plan, incident response, BCP)
- Legal and regulatory compliance requirements (intellectual property, applicable privacy laws)
- Contractual obligation expectations (SLAs, support commitments)

### 5. Project Delivery & Deadlines
Defines delivery and ongoing service expectations:
- Delivery timeline within the specified contract period
- Support model (dedicated representative, response times)
- Ongoing maintenance commitments (e.g., quarterly updates)
- Duration of the overall contract term

## Writing Style Guidelines
- Use formal, neutral, third-person language ("The vendor must...", "The system shall support...")
- Each requirement should be specific and testable
- Group related requirements under clear subheadings
- Keep each section focused only on its purpose
